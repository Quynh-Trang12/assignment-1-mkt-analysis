## THE MARKET OFFERING

Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags (Trà Đen Nhãn Vàng Cozy) are a premium-quality packaged black tea product manufactured by Công ty Thế Hệ Mới (Future Generation Co., Ltd.), a Vietnamese tea enterprise founded in 1996 and established the Cozy consumer brand in 2003. The product analyzed is the 50-gram retail format, containing 25 individually wrapped tea bags of 2 grams each, retailed at Vietnamese supermarkets and online marketplaces. The product is sold at Lotte Mart Đà Nẵng (Unit 1F-09, 06 Nại Nam Street) at 34,300 Vietnamese Đồng (VND), positioning it as a mass-market, value-oriented choice within Vietnam's growing domestic tea segment.

The market offering provides multiple layers of benefit to consumers. Functionally, Cozy Yellow Label delivers 100 percent pure black tea in a convenient, portion-controlled tea-bag format, enabling consistent and easy brewing at home or in commercial beverage settings (Cozy, 2023). The product is explicitly marketed for both household consumption and professional pha chế (beverage preparation) applications, such as fruit tea and milk tea preparation, thereby broadening its appeal beyond individual consumers to small cafés and food service operations (Cozy, 2023). Socially, the product carries the prestige of a domestically produced Vietnamese brand with transparent plantation sourcing across multiple Vietnamese tea-growing regions (Thái Nguyên, Hà Giang, Điện Biên, Phú Thọ, Yên Bái) and certification to international standards (ISO 22000, HACCP, GMP, Halal). Psychologically, the brand emphasizes emotional comfort and cultural identity, inviting consumers to experience "những phút giây thư giãn" (moments of relaxation) while supporting a national tea producer that has invested in quality control, export infrastructure, and equipment modernization (Cozy, 2023). This combination of functional reliability, social acceptability, and emotional resonance makes Cozy a compelling everyday choice for Vietnamese households and small businesses seeking trusted, affordable tea.

## THE MARKETING MIX

The marketing mix framework, commonly expressed as the four Ps—Product, Price, Place, and Promotion—represents a foundational strategic approach to aligning organizational capabilities with customer needs (Armstrong et al., 2021). Beyond its role as a tactical checklist, the marketing mix functions as a lens through which firms segment markets, position offerings, and coordinate resources to create defensible competitive advantage (Constantinides, 2006). For a packaged consumer good such as Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags, each element of the marketing mix is calibrated to reach price-sensitive, convenience-focused Vietnamese households while simultaneously maintaining margins and supporting a mid-market quality positioning.

### Product

The product dimension encompasses the core, actual, and augmented levels of the offering. At the core level, consumers purchase pure black tea—a beverage base chosen for flavor depth, household familiarity, and suitability for mixing with fruit, sugar, milk, or honey. The actual product is the 50-gram box containing 25 pre-portioned tea bags, each sealed in a paper envelope for freshness and ease of handling. The yellow-gold colored box, dark typography, and Cozy branding create immediate visual recognition on the retail shelf and differentiate the product from competitors such as Lipton and Vinatea, which employ cooler, less visually prominent packaging. At the augmented level, Cozy emphasizes manufacturing pedigree and certifications: tea sourced from company-controlled plantations spanning more than 4,500 hectares across Vietnam, production and packaging using technology from Germany and Italy, and compliance with ISO 22000 (food safety management), HACCP (hazard analysis), GMP (good manufacturing practice), and Halal certification standards (Cozy, 2023). This augmented positioning signals quality, safety, and traceability to consumers concerned with product integrity and environmental or religious sourcing standards.

### Price

Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags is priced at 34,300 VND for the 50-gram (25-bag) format at Lotte Mart Đà Nẵng, positioning the product within a competition-based pricing strategy. This price is approximately 14 percent below the mainstream global brand Lipton (39,800 VND) and approximately 13 percent below the Vietnamese national brand Vinatea (44,500 VND), while remaining significantly below premium imports such as Dilmah (72,600 VND) or heritage premium brands like Twinings (204,359 VND). This pricing reflects a value-market positioning: affordable enough for daily household consumption and high-volume commercial use, yet supported by quality claims and certifications that justify a premium relative to unbranded or no-name tea products. The pricing strategy is consistent with FMCG best practices for emerging markets, where competitive pricing combined with visible quality assurance (certifications, brand recognition, modern packaging) drives purchase intent among middle-income and price-conscious consumers (Büyükdağ et al., 2020). Price promotions and multi-pack bundles are strategically deployed in supermarkets and online marketplaces to capture impulse buyers and encourage trial among households unfamiliar with the Cozy brand.

### Place

Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags employs a hybrid distribution strategy spanning modern retail, online marketplaces, and direct-to-consumer channels. Modern trade channels include Lotte Mart and AEON hypermarkets, which stock the product in the dry goods and hot-beverages aisle at eye-level to slightly below eye-level positions—a shelf placement strategy that maximizes visibility for a mid-tier, price-conscious product segment and encourages price-comparison shopping (Han et al., 2022). E-commerce distribution includes major Vietnamese marketplaces (Shopee, Lazada), where the product benefits from search discoverability, customer reviews, and convenient home delivery—critical for urban and time-constrained consumers (Rajaguru et al., 2024). A direct-to-consumer presence on the company website (cozy.vn) provides brand authority and links to marketplace storefronts, reinforcing the brand's quality messaging and enabling distribution control across channels. This multi-channel approach aligns with modern consumer expectations for convenience and choice while maintaining consistent brand positioning and pricing across channels.
 Price promotions and multi-pack bundles are strategically deployed in supermarkets and online marketplaces to capture impulse buyers and encourage trial among households unfamiliar with the Cozy brand (Karpyn et al., 2020; Slapø et al., 2021).
### Promotion

Promotional activities for Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags emphasize in-store visibility, value positioning, and digital engagement. At point of sale, eye-level shelf blocks (2–3 facings per planogram position), shelf strips highlighting multi-pack offers (e.g., "25-bag + bonus pack"), and temporary price promotions (e.g., "3 boxes for 99,900 VND") drive attention and purchase impulse among supermarket shoppers. Online marketplaces employ keyword search optimization, product detail pages with customer reviews and ratings, and promotional banners ("Best Seller," discounts tied to seasonal campaigns) to increase visibility and conversion. Digital and social media promotion through the Cozy brand's presence on Instagram, Shopee, and Lazada reinforce brand identity, communicate new product variants, and engage younger consumer segments who value convenience and local brands. The promotion mix reflects the reality that packaged tea in the Vietnamese market is a low-involvement, habitual purchase category in which consumers are responsive to price visibility and convenience cues but less likely to undertake extensive search or deliberative comparison (Verplanken & Orbell, 2003; Iyer et al., 2019; Palalić et al., 2020).

## MAPPING MY DECISION-MAKING

Consumer decision-making for packaged goods such as tea typically follows a five-stage model in which individuals recognize a need, search for information, evaluate alternatives, make a purchase decision, and reflect on their choice (Armstrong et al., 2021). This model, though sometimes simplified for habitual, low-involvement purchases, provides a useful framework for understanding how shopper behavior unfolds in real retail environments.

**Stage 1: Need Recognition.** I recognize the need for Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags when I realize I am running low on tea supplies at home, or when I wish to stock a variety of tea for different beverage preparations (fruit tea, milk tea, or simple hot tea for study or relaxation). The need is triggered by an internal cue (desire for a familiar beverage or comfort ritual) and reinforced by external context (entering a supermarket for weekly shopping, noticing an empty tea shelf at home, or hearing peers discuss their favorite teas at university or social settings).

**Stage 2: Information Search.** As I navigate the tea aisle at Lotte Mart Đà Nẵng, I conduct an internal information search, recalling that Cozy is a trusted, widely available Vietnamese brand. I may also scan competing brands (Lipton, Phúc Long, Vinatea, Dilmah) by reading package labels, comparing the number of bags per box, and noting price tags. I may glance at customer reviews on the shelf or on my mobile phone (via Lotte Mart's app or Shopee/Lazada if shopping online), and I might ask peers or family members whether they have tried the product.

**Stage 3: Evaluation of Alternatives.** In this stage, I weigh Cozy against competing tea brands using criteria that matter most to me: affordability (Cozy at 34,300 VND is cheaper than Lipton at 39,800 VND and far cheaper than Dilmah at 72,600 VND), convenience (all competitors offer tea-bag formats, but Cozy's packaging design and 25-bag count feels practical for my weekly shopping), familiarity (Cozy's presence in multiple supermarkets and online marketplaces signals reliability), and perceived quality (the visible certifications, Vietnamese origin story, and competitive price combination suggest good value rather than premium or budget compromise).

**Stage 4: Purchase Decision.** For me, purchasing Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags is a repeat purchase decision, not a trial or switching decision. Because I have already used and accepted the product, and because the shopping context is stable (same supermarket, same product location, similar price and promotional conditions), my purchase decision is largely habitual and influenced by availability and habit strength rather than deliberate evaluation (Danner et al., 2008; Ji et al., 2007; Neale et al., 2011). I would place one or two boxes of Cozy in my shopping basket, particularly if the shelf is well-stocked and no out-of-stock situations or unexpected competitive promotions disrupt my routine. However, unexpected situational factors—such as a temporary price cut on Lipton, a stock-out of Cozy, or a roommate's suggestion of an alternative—could alter my final decision.

**Stage 5: Post-Purchase Behaviour.** After purchase and consumption, I evaluate whether Cozy met my expectations regarding flavour strength, ease of brewing, value for money, and suitability for my preferred beverage preparations. If the tea delivers a rich, consistent flavour (as advertised: "đậm đà"/full-bodied), if the tea bags are robust and easy to use, and if the price-per-bag feels fair, I am likely to feel satisfied and repurchase the product on my next shopping trip. Conversely, if the flavour is weak or the packaging feels cheap, I may experience dissatisfaction and seek alternatives or defer the category entirely.

## MY CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

Customer experience (CX) encompasses the cumulative impression and emotional response a consumer develops through interactions with a product, brand, and retail environment (Gahler et al., 2023). Within retail settings, the sensory dimension of CX—the degree to which the environment deliberately engages sight, sound, touch, smell, and taste—plays a significant role in shaping attention, emotion, and purchase likelihood (Spence et al., 2014). For Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags, my retail experience at Lotte Mart Đà Nẵng was notably shaped by a coherent, multisensory retail environment that made the product easy to choose and emotionally satisfying to purchase.

Visually, the beverage aisle at Lotte Mart features bright, even lighting and systematic shelving that makes products easy to scan and compare. The Cozy Yellow Label box employs a bold yellow-gold colour block with dark typography—a design choice that makes it more visually salient than the cooler-toned competitors (Lipton's red and white, Vinatea's green) surrounding it on the shelf. This visual contrast functions as a sensory cue that draws attention and facilitates recognition (Spence et al., 2014; Petit et al., 2019), making the product easier to locate on subsequent shopping trips and reducing cognitive load for a low-involvement purchase category.

Auditorily, the store environment includes soft background music (light pop or instrumental pieces), occasional Vietnamese public announcements, and the low, continuous ambient sound of shopping carts and customer movement. This auditory landscape creates a sense of controlled activity—busy enough to feel like a thriving, well-stocked retailer, yet calm enough to avoid sensory overload or negative affect (Doucé et al., 2020; Yoganathan et al., 2019). The beverage aisle itself is quieter than the front-of-store areas, which may further support an impression of calm, practical shopping for daily staples.

Tactually, when I picked up the Cozy box, I noticed the cardboard felt sturdy yet lightweight, and the box shape allowed easy one-handed carrying. Inside, I could sense the individual tea bags through the carton, suggesting convenience and single-serve control. The physical design reinforces the product's promise of ease and practicality—important attributes for a student or busy household seeking a straightforward, time-efficient beverage solution.

Olfactorily, the tea aisle itself carries a clean, neutral scent profile—distinct from the richer, more indulgent aromas emanating from nearby bakery, coffee, and fresh-produce sections. This olfactory separation may subtly position tea as a practical, everyday category rather than an indulgent or premium treat, which aligns with Cozy's value positioning and accessibility. The lack of competing food scents near the tea shelves also allows the functional and practical aspects of the purchase decision to dominate, rather than triggering emotion-driven or impulse-based selection.

Gustatively, the packaging and brand messaging emphasize flavour depth ("đậm đà"/full-bodied black tea) and suitability for mixing into fruit tea or milk tea, which are popular, familiar beverage categories in Vietnam. Although I have not yet consumed the product during this shopping trip, the promise of a rich, satisfying flavour is communicated through the brand narrative and the product description on the packaging, contributing to a favourable anticipated experience.

Overall, the sensory coherence of the Lotte Mart environment—clear visual differentiation, calm auditory context, intuitive tactile design, neutral olfactory context, and a promised gustative satisfaction—combined to make Cozy Yellow Label Black Tea Bags an intuitive, low-friction choice. The multisensory alignment reduced my cognitive effort, increased my affective response (comfort, confidence), and reinforced my purchase intention and repurchase likelihood (Duong et al., 2022; Haritaoglu et al., 2022).
