Assessment explanation

Task

For your first assignment, you'll be undertaking an Applied Consumer Analysis. This assignment will involve you visiting a local retail store and selecting a product for the basis of the assignment. You will then complete a written portfolio which will see you applying some marketing principles to your chosen product and your experience as a consumer.

Instructions

**Store Visit**

You will need to firstly visit any **physical** **store** (e.g. a grocery store, supermarket, a beauty or sports retailer etc). When you’re in-store, take some time to browse their available products. After walking around, you will select **one** product to complete your assignment and take a photo of it in-store. A tangible/physical ‘good’ which is sold by a brand is highly recommended (e.g. a chocolate bar, protein powder, lipstick etc). You are **not required** to purchase the product.

You should be mindful of your senses while in store, how you feel and think as you walk around, and any interactions with employees. You might like to take some notes on these aspects while you’re there which you can refer to later. You should take some notes of packaging or product information that is present with the product to inform your assignment, or at least take some photos of it to refer to later if needed.  You are of course welcome to revisit the store as you’re completing the assignment to collect more information.

**Tips on product selection**

* Do not select a product sold online (e.g. listed on Amazon).  This assignment intends to draw upon your physical in-store experience.
* Select a product which is tangible (e.g. a chocolate bar), not a service.
* Select a product which interests you, as this will feed into later assignments.
* Try not to select a product or brand that is too obscure or niche, as you will be conducting some research on it. To make your life easier, select a product offered by a brand that is relatively well known.
* Try not to select a product which is overly technical (e.g. products like smartphones, laptops, household appliances, video games or consoles etc). The product you choose in this assignment will feed into future assignments about planning and marketing a start-up which sells this product. The simpler the product you choose in this assignment, the better.

**The Written Assignment Structure and Tasks**

**Cover Page**

At the front of your assignment, there should be a cover page. This clear and professional cover page should include your name, student number, unit code and title, teacher name, and assessment title. If you have used GenAI, include your statement on how it was used here (see our [Use of Generative AI](https://swinburne.instructure.com/courses/75910/pages/use-of-generative-ai-genai-in-this-unit) page for more information).

Then, the written assignment is to be structured under four main sections. Use distinct headings to indicate these sections.

**Section 1** **– The Market Offering** *(approx. 150 - 200 words)*

In this section, you’ll provide context on the selected product.

* Provide the name of the product and its associated producer/brand (e.g. Matcha Powder by T2 Tea)
* Provide the seller/retailer of the product (e.g. Woolworths).
* Attach at least one photo you’ve taken of the product on the shelf or how it's otherwise displayed in-store. Please avoid taking photographs of customers or employees, just focus on your chosen product.
* Explain the benefit or benefits which would be provided to you by using the product (e.g. functional, social, or psychological benefits)

**Section 2** – **The Marketing Mix** *(approx. 600 - 800 words)*

In this section, you’ll analyse your chosen product using the marketing mix (4P’s).

* Explain the marketing mix and its importance for marketers, using a credible reference. Briefly describe each element of the marketing mix.
* Then, analyse the chosen product using the marketing mix (4Ps):
  + **Product**: Analyse and describe the product characteristics using the three-levels of products (core customer value, actual product, augmented product).
  + **Price:** Analyse and describe the pricing strategy of the product:
    - What is the price of the product?
    - Which pricing strategy might the product be using, and why?
  + **Placement**: Analyse and describe the distribution strategy (Placement) of the product, considering how it reaches the consumer and where it is sold:
    - Whether the product's producer uses a direct or indirect marketing channel to get it to the consumer. Explain your reasoning.
      * *Remember, you’re taking the perspective of the product’s producer/brand in how they've chosen to engage in placement – not necessarily the retailer who is selling the product.*
    - How is the product placed within the store itself for convenience or to attract attention?
  + **Promotion**: Analyse and describe any current promotional tools used for the product. This should consider advertising, sales promotions, and other associated promotional efforts you can find which are currently active, either in-store or within the broader market for your product.

***Reminder****: you’ll be analysing the marketing mix of your chosen product, not the marketing mix of the retailer you visited. For example, if your chosen product was a Cadbury Dairy Milk Chocolate Bar being sold at Woolworths, you would focus on analysing the marketing mix relating to the Cadbury Dairy Milk Chocolate Bar,****not****Woolworths.*

**Section 3 – Mapping my Decision-making** *(approx. 200 - 300 words)*

In this section, you will demonstrate your understanding of consumer decision-making and create a visual map that shows how you, as a consumer, would move through each stage of the decision-making process for your chosen product.

* Define and explain the consumer-decision making process using any appropriate references. You should briefly describe each phase of the process to demonstrate your understanding.
* Create a simple visual decision-making map that includes all stages of the consumer decision-making process if you would purchase the product. Use icons or images to represent your decision-making at each stage, with very minimal text in the visual decision-making map to give context to these icons or images.
  + *For example, you might use an icon or image symbolising hunger in the ‘need recognition’ stage, or images of other products you might consider in the ‘evaluation of alternatives’ stage.*
* Consider the following when filling out your map:
  + **Need Recognition**: What is the need or problem that would motivate you to want to purchase the product?
  + **Information Search**: Which sources would you seek out to find information about the product? Friends, family, online, offline sources, past experiences?
  + **Evaluation of Alternatives**: Which other brands or products would you consider? What criteria would be most important to you?
  + **Purchase Decision**: Where and how would you buy the product? Would it be a trial, repeat, or long-term commitment purchase?
  + **Post-Purchase Behaviour**: Would you be dissatisfied, satisfied, or delighted? Would you recommend it to others, complain, or avoid or purchase the product again?
* Underneath the map, provide a discussion. Walk through each phase of your decision-making process and give additional context on what you did or would do if deciding to purchase the product.

***Reminder****: You are not being graded on your design skills when putting together the map. What’s more important is that each phase of the decision-making process is clearly visualised and then discussed.*

If you're not sure how you should go about visualising your decision-making map in Section 3, [here is a resource to get you started](https://swinburne.instructure.com/courses/75910/files/43168950?wrap=1)[Download here is a resource to get you started](https://swinburne.instructure.com/courses/75910/files/43168950/download?download_frd=1).

**Section 4 – My Customer Experience***(approx. 300 words)*

In this section, you’ll discuss your customer experience (CX) while you were in-store. This is where taking notes on your in-store experience will come in handy.

* Choose **one**of the following CX dimensions: Sensorial, emotional, relational, cognitive, physical, symbolic.
* Provide a brief description of the CX dimension you chose to focus on, including a reference.
* Discuss your personal experience of this CX dimension when you were in-store.
  + *For example, if you chose the sensorial CX dimension you would discuss how your senses were stimulated when navigating the store.*
