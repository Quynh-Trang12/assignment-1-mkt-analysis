Customer centricity
Companies used to be more product-centric, with their attention placed on
creating superior products and production efficiencies (Shah et al., 2006).
Customer centricity is the practice of putting the consumer at the heart
of company operations and putting their experience first.
• “What the customer thinks [they are] buying … determines what a
business is, what it produces and whether it will prosper. The customer
is the foundation of a business and keeps it in existence.” (Drucker, 2007,
p. 47-48)
• Companies should not focus on merely selling products but rather on
fulfilling customer needs
Consumers increasingly expect positive customer experiences.
Drucker, P. (2007). The practice of management, Taylor & Francis Group.
Shah, D., Rust, R. T., Parasuraman, A., Staelin, R., & Day, G. S. (2006). The path to customer centricity. Journal of Service Research, 9(2), 113-124.

The Customer Experience (CX)
Customer experience is a “customer’s subjective, directed, and
multidimensional mental responses to an interaction with an experience
partner at a touchpoint in a customer journey stage” (Gahler et al., 2023, p. 194)
Customer experience (CX) is the internal and subjective response customers
have to any direct or indirect interaction with a company.
Companies act as ‘experience stagers’, not just offering goods or services but
the resulting experience, rich with sensations, created within each customer.
Experiences are memorable and internalised by individuals. They resonate long
after the experience has ended.

The touchpoint is the point of interaction
“Touchpoints reflect the array of individual contacts between the
brand/firm and customers across the customer journey that serve a
purpose such as information gathering, payment, unpacking, and
usage” (De Keyser et al., 2020, p. 438)
When moving through the customer journey, customers interact
with touchpoints either directly or indirectly, which informs their
overall customer experience.
De Keyser, A., Verleye, K., Lemon, K. N., Keiningham, T. L., & Klaus, P. (2020). Moving the customer experience field forward: introducing the touchpoints, context, qualities (TCQ) nomenclature. Journal of Service Research, 23(4), 433-455.
Lemon, K. N., & Verhoef, P. C. (2016). Understanding customer experience throughout the customer journey. Journal of Marketing, 80(6), 69-96.

Touchpoints are conveyed through channels
A channel is the medium or route through which interactions between the
customer and the company occur. Channels act as the conduits for
touchpoint interactions.
• A brand’s website (touchpoint) accessed through the internet (channel)
• A pamphlet (touchpoint) sent by mail (channel)
• An advertisement (touchpoint) on the side of a bus (channel)
• Talking to sales staff (touchpoint) at a physical store location (channel)
Channels define the opportunity or the constraint around a touchpoint.

Touchpoints connect over time to create journeys
Touchpoint interactions can be organised to reflect a journey.
Pre-purchase – What interactions aided the customer’s initial
awareness of the company or a product? A customer may interact
with touchpoints such as advertising, social media, online reviews,
or word of mouth.
Purchase – What interactions facilitated the purchase? A customer
may interact with touchpoints such as their website, physical store,
or sales staff.
Post-purchase – What interactions happens once the purchase is
complete? A customer may interact with touchpoints such as
customer support for queries or returns, email newsletters,
customer feedback surveys etc.

Touchpoints create a customer journey
Touchpoints collectively create a customer journey — the
sequence of interactions a customer has with a company.
“The customer purchase journey [is] the process a customer
goes through, across all stages and touch points, that makes up
the customer experience” (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016, p. 71).
The customer experience is constructed through interactions
with touchpoints as the consumer moves through their
purchase journey.
Lemon, K. N., & Verhoef, P. C. (2016). Understanding customer experience throughout the customer journey. Journal of Marketing, 80(6), 69-96.
Qualtrics. (2020, October 20). Understanding customer journey touchpoints. https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/customer-experience/how-to-understand-touchpoints-on-a-customer-journey-map/

Customer journey mapping
• A customer journey map outlines the stages of a
customer’s relationship with the company.
• It breaks each stage into steps, showing the customer’s
experience from their perspective and identifying key
interactions throughout the journey.
• The map captures customers’ feelings, actions, and pains
when they interact with company touchpoints.
• Once these insights are clear, companies can design an
ideal experience based on the customer’s viewpoint.
• Multiple maps might be created to cater for various types
of customer journeys.
Villani, I. (2018). Transform customer experience: How to achieve customer success and create exceptional CX. John Wiley & Sons, Incorporated.

Key elements of a customer journey map
• The customer profile – whose journey are we mapping?
• Purchase phases (pre-purchase, purchase, post-purchase), which
group the following information across the customer journey:
• Touchpoints and customer actions – covered previously
• Pain points
• Customer experience (thoughts and feelings)
• Opportunities

The customer profile
A customer profile is a detailed description of a customer or group of customers, based on data.
A profile may be constructed through collecting different types of information about them:
• Demographic information: age, gender, ethnicity, income, education etc.
• Psychographic information : Attitudes, opinions, values and beliefs, hobbies and interests,
lifestyle etc.
• Geographic information : Location of residence (country, state, local levels), urban vs rural
location, population density etc.
• Behavioural information: Buying patterns, spending habits, brand interactions, how they use
your products or services etc.
• A customer profile acts as a comprehensive snapshot that can give you insights into a
customer group. This data is typically sourced from website or app interactions, purchase
history, social media engagement, customer surveys, and more.
Haan, Wl. & Main, K. (2024, May 28). Customer profile template and examples. Forbes. https://www.forbes.com/advisor/business/customer-profile-template/

Customer journey phases
Pre-purchase Purchase Post-purchase
Awareness Consideration Purchase Retention Advocacy
Pre-purchase
• Awareness: This is the first stage of the customer journey, where a customer becomes aware of a company, product,
or service.
• Consideration: Once customers know about your product or service, they start their research and compare brands.
Purchase
• Purchase: The customer chooses a solution and is ready to buy your product or service.
Post-purchase
• Retention: After the purchase, it's about retaining that customer and nurturing a relationship.
• Advocacy: The customer not only continues to buy your product but acts as an advocate, recommending it to others.
