Marketing is everywhere, and it’s changing

Marketing can be easily misunderstood

Many think marketing = just selling & advertising.

Marketing is much broader and more strategic.

Marketing is about creating and capturing value

• Focus on satisfying customer needs, not just making a sale

• Understand customer needs → develop offerings (products, services, experiences) →

deliver superior value to customers (greater perceived benefits relative to the competition)

• In return, companies capture customer value in the form of profit and customer equity

• Many definitions of marketing, this is just one from the Armstrong textbook:

“Marketing is the process by which marketing organisations engage customers, build strong customer

relationships and create customer value in order to capture value from customers in return” (Armstrong

et al., 2021, p .5)

The Marketplace

• A market is the set of actual and potential buyers of a product. These buyers share a particular

need or want that can be satisfied through exchange relationships.

• The main elements of a marketing system include, suppliers, the company, competitors,

marketing intermediaries, and consumers. Environmental forces also influence the marketplace.

• The marketplace is comprised of exchanges and transactions.

Exchange: the act of obtaining a desired object from

someone by offering something in return.

Transaction: a trade between two parties that involves at

least two things of value, agreed-upon conditions, and a

time and place of agreement.

Understanding needs, wants, and demands

Needs: States of felt deprivation.

Wants: The form human needs take, as shaped by culture and individual personality.

Demands: Human wants that are backed by buying power.

Marketers fulfil consumer wants and needs through market offerings: goods,

services, information, or experiences offered to a market to satisfy a need or a want.

Designing marketing strategy

Hinges on understanding:

• What customers will we serve (our ‘target market’)

• Market segmentation

• Selecting market segments we know we can serve well, and hopefully profitably (targeting)

• How can we serve these customers best (our ‘value proposition’)

• The set of benefits or values a company promises to deliver to consumers to satisfy their needs.

Value propositions differentiate one brand from another

Actioning strategy via the marketing mix

• The marketing strategy to communicate value to customers is put

into action via the marketing mix

• The marketing mix is the set of marketing tools the company uses to

implement its marketing strategy

• The marketing mix is also known as the 4P’s of Marketing

• The Marketing Mix (4Ps): Product, Price, Placement, Promotion

Creating lasting relationships with customers

Customer relationship management is central to modern marketing – it’s the process of

building and maintaining profitable customer relationships by delivering superior customer

value and satisfaction. How can we acquire, keep, and grow our customer base?

• Customer-perceived value: The customer’s evaluation of the difference between benefits

and costs of a marketing offer relative to competitors.

• Customer satisfaction: The extent to which a product’s perceived performance matches

or exceeds a customer’s expectations.

Capturing customer value in return is … valuable

Reason #1: Customer loyalty and retention

It’s cheaper to keep an old customer than to acquire a new customer – keeping your

existing customers loyal is critical.

Losing a customer is more than losing a single sale – it’s losing all the potential purchases

they would have made over their patronage lifetime.

We want to maximise our customer lifetime value (CLV) – the total profit a company

expects to earn from a customer over their entire relationship, after subtracting the costs

of attracting and serving that customer.

Capturing customer value in return is … valuable

Reason #2: Share of customer

• The portion of the customer's purchasing that a company gets in its product categories

• E.g. Amazon starting out selling books online, now offers massive range of products, plus subscription services

(Amazon Prime, Amazon Music) – capturing a greater share of each customer’s spending budget.

Reason #3: Customer equity

• The total combined customer lifetime value of all the company’s customers – both current

and potential. Basically, what is the future value of the company’s customer base?

• Remember – CLV is built through loyalty! More loyalty = more customer equity

A product is a good, a service, or an idea received in an exchange.

Therefore, anything capable of satisfying a want or a need can be called a product.

• Products are tangible or intangible benefits we pay for.

Product

A consumer product is a product bought by final consumers for personal consumption. Consumer

products can be further classified, based on how consumers go about buying them, as:

• Convenience products – Frequent purchase, little planning, little comparison or shopping effort,

low customer involvement

• Shopping products – Less frequent purchase, much planning and shopping effort, comparison of

brands on price, quality, style

• Specialty products – Strong brand preference and loyalty, special purchase effort, little comparison

of brands, low price sensitivity

• Unsought products – Little product awareness, knowledge (or, if aware, little or even negative

interest)

Product: Three Levels of Products

Marketers think about products on three levels:

• Core customer value/product: what is the customer really buying?

• Actual product: how the core benefit is delivered to the consumer

• Augmented product: additional services and benefits

Price

• Price is the amount of money charged for a product or service, or the sum

of the values consumers exchange for the benefits of having or using the

product or service.

• Basically, what am I exchanging of value to receive the benefit of a product or service?

• Price is the only element in the marketing mix that produces revenue; all

other elements represent costs

• Price and value are not the same

Price: Considerations for pricing

• The price the company charges will fall somewhere between one

that is too high to produce any demand and one that is too low

to produce a profit

• The price should be below the price ceiling – the limit to what a

consumer thinks is worth paying given the value they perceive

towards the product

• The price should be above the price floor – the minimum which

can be charged that allows the company to make a profit

• The price is also affected by competitors, elements of the

marketing mix, and the broader market

There are three main pricing strategies:

• customer value-based pricing, cost-based pricing, competition-

based pricing.

Price: Strategies

Customer value-based pricing strategy

Customer value-based pricing refers to the process of setting the price based on

buyers’ perceptions of value, rather than on the seller’s costs.

Price: Strategies

Cost-based pricing strategy

Cost-based pricing refers to the process of setting prices based on the costs for producing, distributing

and selling the product, plus a fair rate of return for company’s effort and risk.

• Fixed costs (overhead) are those costs that do not vary with production or sales level. E.g. rent,

salaries for a restaurant.

• Variable costs are those costs that vary directly with the level of production. E.g. preparing more

restaurant meals means more costs incurred from ingredients, takeaway packaging etc.

Price: Strategies

Competition-based pricing

• Competition-based pricing refers to the process of setting prices based on competitors’ strategies,

costs, prices and market offerings.

• Consumers base their judgments of a product’s value on the prices that competitors charge for

similar products.

•

•

If customers perceive greater value, a company can justify a higher price.

If customers perceive less value, the firm may need to charge a lower price or change perceptions

by improving or communicating value.

Placement

How does the product get from our suppliers to the consumer?

A supply chain is a system of efficiently and effectively producing, making and getting products to

end-users. A supply chain pertains to both physical goods and services.

Placement

Marketing logistics (physical distribution) are the tasks involved in planning, implementing

and controlling the physical flow of materials, final goods and related information from points

of origin to points of consumption in order to meet customer requirements at a profit.

• How can we profitably turn materials into products and get these to consumers?

• Warehousing: Storing goods prior to their sale.

•

Inventory management: Ensuring we have enough stock to meet demand, but not too

much that we incur extra costs in storage or risk our stock becoming expired or obsolete.

• Transportation: Truck, rail, water, pipeline, air, the internet.

Placement: Marketing channels

Marketing Channels

A marketing channel (or distribution channel) is a set of

interdependent organisations involved in the process of making a

product or service available to users.

Marketing channels are part of the broader supply chain, but focus

specifically on how products move from producers to end users.

Channel members add value by reducing complexity, handling

large volumes, and making products easier to buy.

Placement: Direct vs indirect marketing channels

Direct

Indirect

Marketing channels involve the potential interaction of intermediaries to

bring finished products (goods or services) to the marketplace.

A direct marketing channel has no intermediaries. The producer /

manufacturer sells straight to the consumer

• Producer: Creates or makes the product

•

E.g. a consumer buying sneakers direct from Nike outlet or online store

An indirect marketing channel contains one or more intermediaries.

• Wholesalers: Buy products in bulk from producers and sells to retailers

•

E.g. a wholesaler buying 10,000 pairs of Nike sneakers to then sell to retailers like Culture

Kings, Hype DC

• Retailers: Sell products directly to consumers in smaller quantities

•

e.g. a consumer buying Nike sneakers through retailers such as Culture Kings, Hype DC

Promotion

The promotion mix (or marketing communications mix), consists of the

specific blend of advertising, public relations, personal selling, sales

promotion and direct and digital marketing tools that the company uses

to persuasively communicate customer value and build customer

relationships.

The promotions mix includes:

• Advertising

• Sales promotion

• Personal selling

• Public relations

• Direct and digital marketing

Promotion: The Promotion Mix

Advertising

• Any paid form of non-personal presentation and promotion of ideas, goods or services by an

identified sponsor.

• Advertising includes broadcast, print, internet, outdoor and other forms.

Sales Promotion

• Short-term incentives to encourage the purchase or sale of a product or service.
• Discounts, coupons, displays and demonstrations.

Personal selling

• Personal presentation by the firm’s salesforce for the purpose of making sales and building

customer relationships.

• Sales presentations, trade shows and incentive programs.

Public relations

• Building good relations with the company’s various publics by obtaining favourable publicity,

building up a good corporate image and handling or pre-empting unfavourable stories or events.

• Press releases, sponsorships, special events and web pages

Direct and digital marketing

• Interacting directly with carefully targeted individual consumers and communities to both obtain an

immediate response and build lasting customer relationships.

• Direct marketing includes catalogues, telephone marketing, kiosks, and digital marketing covers

ways we try to communicate with consumers via the internet, mobile phones and more.
