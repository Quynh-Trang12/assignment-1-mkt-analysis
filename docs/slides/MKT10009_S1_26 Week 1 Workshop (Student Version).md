About the Unit

Unit Aims
This unit aims to introduce key marketing concepts and their
application to modern business.
Understanding the role of marketing will aid understanding the
interconnections it has with other business disciplines, as well as
providing a strong philosophical foundation for further studies in
marketing. In addition, this unit provides students with the opportunity
to develop generic skills.
The unit provides students with a series of learning opportunities
designed to explore basic business and Marketing concepts from a
variety of viewpoints including theoretical and applied perspectives.

The Marketing Mix
The marketing mix is the set of marketing
tools the company uses to implement its
marketing strategy.
• Product
• Price
• Placement (or place)
• Promotion

Product: Three Levels of Products
Marketers think about products on three levels:
• Core customer value/product: what is the customer really
buying?
• Actual product: how the core benefit is delivered to the
consumer
• Augmented product: additional services and benefits
Core customer value –
connection, entertainment etc
Actual product – Phone style,
camera, software
Augmented product –Support,
delivery, after-sales service

Run an online search for this product, or refer to the JB Hi-Fi

• Core customer value/product: what is the customer really

• Actual product: how is the core benefit delivered to the

• Augmented product: what are the additional services and

Pricing Strategies
Customer value-based pricing strategy: Customer
value-based pricing refers to the process of setting
the price based on buyers’ perceptions of value,
rather than on the seller’s costs.
Cost-based pricing refers to the process of setting
prices based on the costs for producing, distributing
and selling the product, plus a fair rate of return for
company’s effort and risk.
Competition-based pricing refers to the process of
setting prices based on competitors’ strategies,
costs, prices and market offerings.

Pricing Discussion
Next, your group will identify the potential pricing strategy of Bose
QuietComfort Noise Cancelling Headphones.
What price can you find the product being sold for? Have a look at its
price compared to other brands.
Which pricing strategy might the product be using?
• Might Bose have set prices using a value-based strategy – using
consumer perceptions of value?
• Might Bose have set prices using a cost-based strategy – the cost to
produce the product?
• Might Bose have set prices using a competition-based strategy –
based on the competition?
Use your best judgement given the product.

Placement through Marketing Channels
Marketing Channels
A marketing channel (or distribution channel) is a set of interdependent
Direct Indirect
organisations involved in the process of making a product or service
available to users. Marketing channels focus on how products move from
producers to end users.
A direct marketing channel has no intermediaries. The producer /
manufacturer sells straight to the consumer
• Producer: Creates or makes the product
• E.g. Nike produces their own sneakers and sells them through their own
stores or website
An indirect marketing channel contains one or more intermediaries.
• Wholesalers: Buy products in bulk from producers and sells to retailers
• E.g. a wholesaler buying 10,000 pairs of Nike sneakers to then sell to
retailers like Culture Kings
• Retailers: Sell products directly to consumers in smaller quantities
• e.g. a consumer buying Nike sneakers through retailers such as Culture
Kings

Placement Discussion
Does Bose use direct and/or indirect marketing channels to distribute Bose QuietComfort
Noise Cancelling Headphones?
• Can you buy the product directly from Bose or do you have to go through any retailers?
• What advantage is there for Bose in using a direct and/or indirect distribution strategy?

Promotion
The promotion mix (or marketing communications mix), consists of the specific
blend of advertising, public relations, personal selling, sales promotion and
direct and digital marketing tools that the company uses to persuasively
communicate customer value and build customer relationships. The
promotions mix includes:
• Advertising – Any paid form of non-personal presentation and promotion of
ideas, goods or services by an identified sponsor.
• Sales promotion – Short-term incentives to encourage the purchase or sale
of a product or service
• Personal selling – Personal presentation by the firm’s salesforce for the
purpose of making sales and building customer relationships.
• Public relations – Building good relations with the company’s various publics
by obtaining favourable publicity
• Direct and digital marketing – Interacting directly with carefully targeted
individual consumers and communities, both online and offline.

Promotion Discussion

promotions mix Bose would be likely to use to
promote their product.
• Would they run advertisements?
• Would they offer any sales promotions?
• Would they engage in personal selling?
• Might they use public relations activities?
• Would they use any direct or digital marketing
activities?

Assignment 1: Applied Consumer Analysis
Word count: 1500 words (+/- 10%). Words included within tables, figures, the reference list, or
within the appendix are not included within this word count.
Due date: Wednesday 1st April, 11:59pm (Week 5, before the mid-semester break).
This assignment will involve you visiting a local store and selecting a product for the basis of the
assignment. You will then complete a written portfolio which will see you applying some marketing
principles to your chosen product and your experience as a consumer.
Store Visit
• You will need to firstly visit any physical store. When you’re in-store, take some time to browse
their available products.
• After walking around, you will select one product to complete your assignment and take a photo
of it in-store. A tangible/physical ‘good’ which is sold by a brand is highly recommended (e.g. a
chocolate bar, protein powder, lipstick etc). You are not required to purchase the product.
You will then complete a written assignment.

Assignment Structure
Cover Page: Name, student number, unit code and title, teacher name, and assessment title.
Section 1: The Market Offering
In this section, you’ll provide context on the selected product.
• Provide the name of the product and its associated producer/brand (e.g. Matcha Powder by T2
Tea)
• Provide the seller/retailer of the product (e.g. Woolworths).
• Attach at least one photo you’ve taken of the product on the shelf or how it's otherwise
displayed in-store. Please avoid taking photographs of customers or employees, just focus on
your chosen product.
• Explain the benefit or benefits which would be provided to you by using the product (e.g.
functional, social, or psychological benefits)

Assignment Structure
Section 2: The Marketing Mix
In this section, you’ll analyse your chosen product using the marketing mix (4P’s).
• Explain the marketing mix and its importance for marketers, using a credible reference.
Briefly describe each element of the marketing mix.
Then, analyse the chosen product using the marketing mix (4Ps):
Product: Analyse and describe the product characteristics using the three-levels of products
(core customer value, actual product, augmented product).
Price: Analyse and describe the pricing strategy of the product:
• What is the price of the product?
• Which pricing strategy might the product be using, and why?

Assignment Structure
Section 2: The Marketing Mix (continued)
Placement: Analyse and describe the distribution strategy (Placement) of the product,
considering how it reaches the consumer and where it is sold:
• Whether the product's producer uses a direct or indirect marketing channel to get it to
the consumer. Explain your reasoning.
• How is the product placed within the store itself for convenience or to attract attention?
Promotion: Analyse and describe any current promotional tools used for the product. This
should consider advertising, sales promotions, and other associated promotional efforts you
can find which are currently active, either in-store or within the broader market for your
product.

Assignment Structure
Section 3 – Mapping my Decision-making
In this section, you will demonstrate your understanding of consumer decision-making and
create a basic visual map that shows how you, as a consumer, would move through each stage
of the decision-making process for your chosen product.
• Define and explain the consumer-decision making process using any appropriate
references. Briefly describe each phase of the process.
• Create a simple visual decision-making map that includes all stages of the consumer
decision-making process if you would purchase the product. Use icons or images, with very
minimal text. Underneath the map, provide a discussion.
We will cover this content in Week 2.

Assignment Structure
Section 4 – My Customer Experience
In this section, you’ll discuss your customer experience (CX) while you were in-store. This is
where taking notes on your in-store experience will come in handy.
• Choose one of the following CX dimensions: Sensorial, emotional, relational, cognitive,
physical, symbolic.
• Provide a brief description of the CX dimension you chose to focus on, including a reference.
• Discuss your personal experience of this CX dimension when you were in-store.
We will cover this content in Week 3.
