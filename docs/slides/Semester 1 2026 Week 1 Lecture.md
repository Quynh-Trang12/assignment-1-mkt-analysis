Marketing as science
Marketing uses scientific approaches to understand
consumer behaviour and to drive decision-making
• Marketers study behaviour empirically
• We study what people do, not just what they say.
Using diverse approaches such as surveys,
experiments, interviews, field studies, observation,
biometrics (e.g., eye tracking), neuromarketing
• We aim to test what does and doesn’t work using
data
• Uncovering broader patterns – evidence that repeats
across people, contexts, and time
• Using data insights to drive decision-making
• Improving products, brands, and customer
experiences

Marketing as science: Message framing
Which message is more persuasive to you?
• Option A: “Take these vitamins for a stronger immune system.”
• Option B: “Take these vitamins, so you don’t get sick.”
Pain frames are more persuasive (Option B) – we don’t want
to lose our health
Individuals are loss-averse: potential losses hurt more than
potential gains feel good.
• Individuals feel the pain of loss twice as intensively as the
equivalent pleasure of gain
• Marketers leverage FOMO approaches because of this
Ghuman. (2022, Mar 28). Marketing Lessons From The Neuroscience Of Pain. Forbes. https://www.forbes.com/sites/princeghuman/2022/03/28/marketing-lessons-from-the-neuroscience-pain/
Pilat, D. & Krastev, S. (n.d.). Why do we buy insurance?. The Decision Lab. https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/loss-aversion

Marketing as science: psychology of pricing order
When shopping online, do you sort by:
• Option A: low price to high price (ascending order)
• Option B: high price to low price (descending order)?
Price lists in descending order are more likely to attract purchases at
higher price points.
• When offered with more expensive options first, customers feel that they
lose out on quality the further they scanned down the list.
Suk, K., Lee, J., & Lichtenstein, D. R. (2012). The influence of price presentation order on consumer choice. Journal of Marketing Research, 49(5), 708-717.

Unit Aims
This unit aims to introduce key marketing concepts and their
application to modern business.
Understanding the role of marketing will aid understanding the
interconnections it has with other business disciplines, as well as

marketing. In addition, this unit provides students with the opportunity
to develop generic skills.
The unit provides students with a series of learning opportunities
designed to explore basic business and marketing concepts from a
variety of viewpoints including theoretical and applied perspectives.

Our unit uses Assessment for Learning (A4L)
Assessment for Learning, also known as A4L, is the
assessment approach used in this unit.
A4L units are ungraded, meaning there is no final
percentage grade attached to them. They will reflect
as 'Complete' or 'Incomplete' on your academic
record. A4L units do not contribute to your Grade
Point Average (GPA).
There is an emphasis on providing feedback to
inform your future work both in the unit and to
develop your academic skills more broadly.

Suggested strategies for success

content and become very stressed around assessment time.

inform your assessment. Students who don’t attend workshops don’t do as well.
Make connections. You don’t have to go through the unit on your own – form study
groups or just someone else from class to talk to about how you’re both going (or rant
